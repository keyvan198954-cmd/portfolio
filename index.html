HailOwner: a card, an app, a website, 18 languages

My main project. A QR card sits behind a car's windshield. If something is wrong (the car blocks someone in, the lights are on, a door is open), a person nearby scans the card, picks one ready sentence, and the owner gets a push alert. Nobody sees anybody's phone number, and the person who scans needs no app or account. I designed and built the whole product: the idea, the owner app, the server, the scan page, the admin panel, the website and the videos.

The four situations the card is made for (left), and why a card is better than a phone number on a note (right).

The HailOwner app

An Android app for the car owner (Expo / React Native) with its own server. The owner never signs up: installing and opening the app is the whole registration. Below is the real Home screen of the demo app, in English.

The Home screen

Everything the owner needs first, on one screen:

  • A service reminder that is almost due: "BMW X3 — Oil change and service: in 5 days". The app works out the date from the last service and sends notices before it.
  • A parking alert: "Your vehicle is blocking a parking entrance or exit", with the car, its plate and how long ago it was sent. A person who scanned the card sent it, and the owner can answer with one tap.
  • The owner's car below the alerts, with the card status ("1 card suspended") and the tab bar for Home, Alerts, Vehicles, Reminders and Settings.

This is the real Home screen of the demo app, in English. It shows demo data (a sample car and alert). The app is a working demo and is not in an app store yet.

What the owner can do

  • Receive alerts as push notifications that name the vehicle and plate; urgent ones come with a loud sound.
  • Reply in one tap with a ready sentence. No free text either way, so no abuse.
  • Manage vehicles and cards: register a card by scanning its QR; suspend, reactivate, replace or revoke it.
  • Sell a car easily: release ownership in the app and the new owner scans the same card to claim it.
  • Close people: up to two trusted people, told only in a real emergency.
  • Emergency mode (optional): an encrypted medical card, with every view logged.

What is behind it

  • Scan page: the person who scans sees a web page in their language and picks one of 18 ready sentences in 5 categories.
  • Server: Nuxt / h3 API with PostgreSQL, push notifications and a card-sales backend.
  • Admin panel: password plus authenticator code; connected cards with serial numbers, stats, filters, CSV export and an audit log.
  • Privacy rules: no phone numbers shared; a closed set of sentences; limited vehicle information shown to the sender.
  • Quality: automated tests (about 800 server tests at my last count), and a rule that fails the build if any text is missing in any language.

The HailOwner website

A fast marketing website (no framework, no build step). It explains the idea in four steps, compares a card with a number on the glass, answers questions, and sells the cards.

The app behind the site

The website sells the card; the owner app makes it work. The owner scans the card's QR to register a car, and from then on every alert from a passer-by arrives as a push notification in the owner's own language, naming the car and its plate. The owner answers with one tap, adds several cars, suspends or replaces a lost card, hands a card to a new owner when selling the car, sets reminders and can store an encrypted emergency card.

Demo app, not yet in an app store.

How I made it work

  • One source of text per language, and an automated test that fails if a string is missing in any of the 18.
  • Direction is read from the language, not guessed, so Persian, Arabic, Sorani and Urdu mirror correctly.
  • A searchable language picker that shows each language in its own script, so people can find it in any language.
  • Ready-made alert sentences: 18 sentences in 5 categories in all 18 languages, so a sender and an owner who share no language can still understand each other.

Honest note: Persian is my native language, and English and Kurdish are my working languages. Ten of the 18 languages were machine-translated and have not yet been reviewed by native speakers. For a real launch they need a native review.

The AI salesperson (Smart Seller)

A chat assistant that talks to customers in Persian like a professional perfume consultant. It listens to what the customer wants (who it is for, season, occasion, style, how long it should last, budget) and recommends real products.

  • Only real stock: it picks products from the live catalog that are in stock, in the category the customer asked for (a spray never shows under "men's"), and respects the budget.
  • Honest reasons: it explains each choice from the product's own data (notes, family, longevity, seasons), and says so when a category is empty instead of inventing products.
  • Never an error: a rule-based concierge answers by itself, and the AI model is used for open questions, so the customer always gets a useful reply even if the model is unavailable.

For the shop owner

  • Admin dashboard: orders, products (with an AI button that writes promo text), search and filters, the five categories, and the channel links.
  • Inventory: stock goes down automatically for every paid order.
  • Exchange-rate setting for dollars to toman.

The AI manager (Smart Manager)

A chat assistant inside the admin panel that advises the owner from the store's own numbers. The owner can ask in plain language ("what sold best?", "what should I do today?") or press report buttons.

  • Statistics: sales, the checkout funnel (where customers drop off), what customers ask for, popular products, and conversations with the AI salesperson.
  • Advice on stock and catalog: it flags categories with no sellable product or only a few, products without a photo or a price (they cannot be bought), and inventory problems, and suggests what to add or restock.
  • "Today" list: the actions most worth doing now, built from alerts.
  • Works without AI too: a rule-based advisor answers from the data when the model is unavailable, and says so.

AI agents and an admin dashboard for an Instagram page

A working system built with Python, FastAPI and the Claude API. Twelve agents, each with one job. They pass their results to each other, and every draft waits in an approval queue in the admin dashboard for a person to accept, edit or reject.

How the agents connect

Arrows show who passes what to whom. Dashed lines are rules from the Manager.

The agents working together (39 s)

  1. Learn: Scout finds similar pages; Trends studies their posts.
  2. Plan: Analyst finds what works from real numbers; Goals sets targets; Planner builds the weekly calendar.
  3. Create: Designer writes the post; Hooks tests five opening lines.
  4. Talk: Replier, Engage and Collab draft replies, comments and messages.
  5. Decide: a person approves, edits or rejects. Nothing is published without approval.
  6. Loop: insights and the owner's edits flow back, so the agents improve.

What comes out of one cycle: a weekly plan, ready-to-approve posts (slides, caption, hashtags, image), five opening-line options per post, draft replies, draft comments and messages for similar pages, and a growth report built only from real numbers.

The admin dashboard

  • Approval queue for posts, reels, stories, replies, comments and messages.
  • Content: posts, reels and stories (images or video), with manual editing.
  • Day-by-day plan built from a plain-text brief from the owner.
  • Analytics and goals: what worked, scenarios (cautious, middle, optimistic) and targets.
  • Image generation with several keys and fallbacks.

The dashboard shows a real page's content, so I do not show its screens publicly. I can show it on a call.

The Manager agent

The owner talks to the Manager in plain language ("post on Saturday and Monday", "be warmer in replies"). It turns each request into a rule or a task for the right agent, and can run agents when asked. Each agent keeps a short list of the owner's standing rules.

  • Safety rules built in: official Instagram API only; no automatic likes, follows or comments on other pages; a human approves every draft.
  • Honest by design: agents work only from the numbers they are given and say when data is thin.

Status: working on one test page. Live publishing of reels and video stories is still to be tested.
